# Representative problems for discussing the first workflow

Research date: 2026-09-07. Prepared for [Which representative problems should anchor the first workflow?](https://github.com/tylerdurrett/think-bigger/issues/6), following the operator's preference to explore mathematics first and let other directions earn their place. These are planning examples, not selected solution attempts. The two mathematical options have stronger grounding as initial discussion anchors; the other two are leads whose novelty and relevance need more exploration. There is no requirement to retain one example from each area.

This bounded primary-source review builds on [opportunity discovery](opportunity-discovery.md) and [research evaluation](research-evaluation.md). It did not run code, inspect proof certificates, benchmark hardware, or establish a complete contemporary literature review. Search-indexed source snapshots can precede the research date. In particular, record values below are dated findings, not certified September 2026 targets.

## Two mathematical options

### Larger cap sets: a finite object that can be checked exactly

**Question in plain language.** How large a collection of strings of length eight, using only 0, 1, and 2, can we choose without three distinct strings summing to zero in every position modulo three? This is the eight-dimensional cap-set problem. A larger valid collection improves a mathematical lower bound; finding an ordinary valid collection is only practice. FunSearch published a 512-element construction, and a 2025 research paper still reported 512 as the best known lower bound for this dimension. Neither source establishes the record on the research date. [FunSearch paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06924-6), [Greedy capsets, 2025](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/92/ajc_v92_p463.pdf)

**Public starting materials.** The authors provide actual cap sets, their generating functions, and a notebook. Their repository supplies part of the search implementation but omits the language model, execution sandbox, and distributed infrastructure. Reproducing a known construction and reproducing the discovery process are therefore different undertakings. [Official FunSearch repository](https://github.com/google-deepmind/funsearch)

**Success test and its limit.** A separate checker can use exact modular arithmetic to verify every relevant pair's implied third point is absent. Checking a submitted set establishes a finite construction and its size; it does not prove that no larger set exists, nor that the construction is new. The modular-arithmetic characterization is given in the 2025 paper. The proposed independent checking route is a planning inference. [Greedy capsets](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/92/ajc_v92_p463.pdf)

**Why this may expose useful workflow decisions.** It connects a recognizable object, a proven AI-assisted discovery template, and an inspectable success test. The 2025 paper also explains why a seemingly natural deletion heuristic consistently yields smaller sets: an unsuccessful search direction led to a theorem. That makes it a useful example for discussing meaningful partial findings as well as record improvements. [Greedy capsets](https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/92/ajc_v92_p463.pdf)

**Before recommending an attempt.** Refresh the precise dimension's record and equivalent formulations; decide whether the objective is a larger construction, a structural explanation, or a new bound; inspect the reference implementation and estimate an attempt budget. The ambient space here contains 6,561 strings by arithmetic, but the number of possible subsets is vastly larger. Small inputs and cheap candidate checking do not imply cheap discovery. No resource estimate was measured.

### Erdős' minimum overlap problem: measurable progress toward an infinite question

**Question in plain language.** Split the integers from 1 through 2N into two equally sized groups. Count how often each possible difference occurs between a number in one group and a number in the other. How small can the largest of those counts be, relative to N, as N becomes large? The maintained statement records the exact asymptotic constant as open. Improving a rigorous bound can contribute without determining the constant completely. [Minimum overlap problem](https://www.erdosproblems.com/36)

**Public starting materials and AI precedent.** TTT-Discover's January 2026 paper reports an improved upper bound of approximately 0.380876 and releases code and constructions. Its approach optimizes step functions representing a limiting density; an existing mathematical reduction connects such functions to asymptotic bounds. This is a concrete template for computational search followed by mathematical validation. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.16175), [authors' detailed account](https://test-time-training.github.io/discover.pdf), [official repository](https://github.com/test-time-training/discover)

**Success test and its limit.** Exact counting certifies an individual finite partition's overlap. It does not settle an asymptotic statement. For a step-function construction, the mathematical reduction, feasibility constraints, and bound calculation all need checking; floating-point optimization output alone is insufficient. A rational or rigorously bounded certificate is a possible validation route to investigate, not a checked artifact supplied by this report. The public Lean statement likewise does not create a ready checker for arbitrary natural-language proofs. [TTT-Discover's reduction and constraints](https://test-time-training.github.io/discover.pdf), [formal statement](https://google-deepmind.github.io/formal-conjectures/src/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/%C2%AB36%C2%BB/)

**Status uncertainty is material.** The search-indexed maintained page shows a January 23, 2026 edit and a lower bound above 0.379005. An author's public repository claims a stronger lower-bound certificate above 0.38055470. This review did not validate that claim or establish community acceptance. A further indexed author-hosted paper claims an improvement over TTT-Discover's upper bound, but its direct URL returned a landing page during this review. These leads make the older interval unsuitable as a fresh record target. [Maintained statement](https://www.erdosproblems.com/36), [claimed lower-bound certificate](https://github.com/Leeham06972452/erdos-36-lower-bound), [upper-bound paper lead](https://www.wq-will.com/assets/Evaluation-driven_Scaling_for_Scientific_Discovery.pdf)

**Why this may expose useful workflow decisions.** It exercises prior-work review, conflicting status evidence, partial contributions, and the bridge from promising computation to a theorem. Before attempting it, establish the current defensible bounds and choose a specific kind of progress. TTT-Discover reports training costs of a few hundred dollars per problem in its setup; that is historical experiment evidence, not a quote or feasibility promise for a new attempt. [TTT-Discover paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.16175)

## Two adjacent leads, retained only if they prove worthwhile

### When does denoising single-cell data make predictions worse?

**Concrete starting observation.** Open Problems' denoising release exposes a quality-control finding that its k-nearest-neighbour smoothing method performs worse than controls on Poisson loss. Public inputs include a roughly 1,000-cell blood dataset; task code, baseline methods, and molecular cross-validation are supplied. The evaluation splits observed molecules and tests predictions against withheld counts because clean expression levels are not directly observed. [Versioned denoising benchmark](https://openproblems.bio/benchmarks/denoising/v1.0.0/)

**Proposed investigation question, not an established gap.** Does that failure arise from normalization or scoring conventions, or is there a reproducible regime where smoothing damages prediction? A useful result could identify a consequential evaluation mistake or a clearly characterized failure condition, potentially improving interpretation of denoising results. A displayed QC finding is not evidence that the benchmark is broken; the cause may already be known.

**What would count.** Reproduce the observation on the pinned release, compare controls, separate development and confirmation data, and test the proposed explanation across relevant datasets. This would support a bounded empirical claim under the molecular-cross-validation assumptions; it would not establish a new biological mechanism. The initial data size suggests modest baselines may be accessible, but matrix operations, pipelines, and broader confirmation remain unmeasured. Existing task issues, normalization choices, original method papers, and statistical validity need review before nomination. This lead explores question development from an observed anomaly rather than merely chasing a higher benchmark score.

### Can an evaluator recognize a correct answer reached through faulty reasoning?

**Concrete starting materials.** ProcessBench provides step-by-step mathematical solutions with expert labels identifying the first erroneous step, plus final-answer correctness and evaluation code. Its original study documented poor transfer of several process reward models to harder mathematics. Those 2024–2025 results are historical evidence of a failure mode, not a description of current models. [Original paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.06559), [official data and code](https://github.com/QwenLM/ProcessBench)

**Proposed investigation question.** For solutions whose final answer is correct but an intermediate argument is wrong, can an evaluator identify the error reliably on unfamiliar problem families, and does that reliability survive harmless changes in wording? A contribution might establish a failure boundary or a validated screening method useful before expensive proof checking. This overlaps mathematics and learned evaluation naturally; it does not require a separate product direction or training a new judge.

**What would count.** Use independently justified error labels, keep entire problem families out of development where appropriate, and measure false acceptance and rejection on the specified population. Benchmark agreement is statistical evidence about those cases, not proof certification for novel mathematics. Before nomination, check whether the relevant subgroup is large enough, whether the question is already answered, whether transformations preserve meaning, and whether annotation errors or training contamination undermine evaluation. Model inference and especially training costs are unknown. This is the least mature lead here: without a sharper unaddressed gap, it should remain an idea rather than an anchor.

## What this evidence enables

The operator can now react to two concrete mathematical problems with different validation burdens, without choosing a universal workflow or committing to solve either. The cap-set example emphasizes an exact finite witness; minimum overlap emphasizes a partial mathematical advance and its certification. The adjacent leads remain optional and can be discarded. Selecting the initial anchors, their intended contribution types, and the acceptable attempt budgets remains a live design decision.

# Documented AI-assisted contributions: precedents for Think Bigger

Research date: 2026-09-07. Prepared for [What can we learn from documented AI-assisted contributions?](https://github.com/tylerdurrett/think-bigger/issues/2).

## Scope and answer

This bounded review covers five cases: three mathematical discoveries, one software contribution, and one mathematical rediscovery that clarifies the difference between correctness and novelty. It examines original papers, released artifacts, and records maintained by the people evaluating or integrating the results. It is not a survey of the latest models or a reproduction study. No proof, notebook, training run, or benchmark was executed here.

The evidence answers the planning question: useful precedents exist for discovering constructions, generating proofs, and improving small computational routines. They support a research process with explicit problem statements, inspectable outputs, and assessment appropriate to the claim. They do not establish that an unfamiliar operator can reliably obtain a novel result without expert interpretation, or that the original discovery systems can all be run on a personal computer.

The accessibility judgments below are inferences from the released materials. “Public verification” means the artifact and checking route are available; it does not mean this report independently verified it. Historical resource descriptions are dated to their papers. Current commercial service entitlements, pricing, and model availability were not audited.

## Cases

### FunSearch: larger cap sets, December 2023

**Problem and contribution.** A cap set is a set of vectors over arithmetic modulo three with no three distinct points on a line. Researchers seek larger constructions and stronger asymptotic bounds. FunSearch found improved constructions, including a size-512 cap set in eight dimensions, and improved lower bounds. This was progress on an open extremal problem, not a complete determination of its optimum. **Roles and validation.** Humans supplied the evaluation function, an initial program, and useful algorithm structure. A pretrained language model proposed code; an evolutionary loop retained candidates based on executable evaluation. The Nature paper reports these results with mathematical reasoning and concrete constructions, rather than just a score on a question-answering benchmark. **Compute.** The reported distributed setup typically used 15 samplers and 150 CPU evaluators, with accelerators serving model inference. That is materially different from one interactive chat. These are the authors’ reported methods and results; this review did not locate a separate replication study. [Original paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06924-6).

**Public materials and solo fit.** The authors release construction functions, numerical sets, notebooks, bin-packing evaluations, and a single-threaded pipeline implementation. The release excludes language models, an execution sandbox, and distributed infrastructure. Its notebooks can run in Colab. Inspecting existing constructions is accessible; adapting the method requires supplying the missing execution components and budgeting search. A public release of results should not be mistaken for reproduction of the original discovery run. [Author repository](https://github.com/google-deepmind/funsearch).

### AlphaEvolve: a matrix multiplication construction, May–June 2025

**Problem and contribution.** The reported discovery is a rank-48 bilinear algorithm for multiplying two 4-by-4 complex matrices, improving the 49-multiplication construction obtained by recursively applying Strassen in that setting. The arithmetic setting and algorithm class matter: the claim is not that every matrix product became faster on every computer. **Roles and validation.** Humans defined the task, initial code, and evaluator; the coding agent evolved search algorithms, whose execution produced tensor decompositions. The technical report describes verifiable mathematical outputs. It also distinguishes discovery from simply matching known constructions. **Compute.** The pipeline uses model sampling and distributed evaluation; the appendix reports GPU evaluation and memory limits for larger matrix sizes. The full cost of rediscovering this particular construction is not established here. [Original technical report](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.13131v1).

**Independent follow-through.** Dumas, Pernet, and Sedoglavic subsequently derived a rational-coefficient version from the AlphaEvolve construction and published its mathematics. This is substantive independent use of the result, rather than repetition of the developer’s announcement. It does not independently reproduce AlphaEvolve’s discovery process. [Follow-up paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13242). The researchers’ catalog supplies algebraic encodings and identifies the original complex-valued algorithm and subsequent variants. [University of Lille algorithm record](https://fmm.univ-lille.fr/4x4x4.html).

**Public materials and solo fit.** DeepMind releases a Colab notebook containing the mathematical results and correctness-checking code; it explicitly does not release the AlphaEvolve runner in that repository. Verification and study are accessible starting points. The released notebook alone is insufficient to reproduce the agent search. No inference about current access to the hosted product follows from that limitation. [Author results repository](https://github.com/google-deepmind/alphaevolve_results).

### AlphaDev: small sorting routines adopted by libc++, 2022–2023

**Problem and contribution.** AlphaDev found faster small sorting routines, which were translated into C++ and incorporated into LLVM’s libc++ library. This is a concrete software contribution, even though performance benchmarks form part of its evidence. **Roles and assessment.** Researchers constructed an assembly-program game and trained a reinforcement-learning agent to generate correct, efficient routines. Humans interpreted the output, translated it, tested it, and integrated it. The paper combines correctness assessment with measured latency; neither the speedups nor hardware behavior should be treated as universal mathematical guarantees. **Compute.** The reported training used up to 16 TPU v3 cores and up to 512 actors on TPU v4, with worst-case convergence of two days across tasks. This is not evidence that the original training fits ordinary personal hardware. [Original Nature paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06004-9).

**Independent integration evidence.** LLVM’s public review records the reinforcement-learning origin, performance measurements on Intel, AMD, and ARM machines, reviewer discussion, test changes, and the landing commit. The review opened in January 2022 and landed in April 2022, before the June 2023 publication. This independently documents adoption and engineering scrutiny; it is not a replication of the training experiment. [LLVM review and commit record](https://reviews.llvm.org/D118029).

**Public materials and solo fit.** The release includes discovered assembly routines and correctness tests, plus agent/environment pseudocode. Linux with Clang and Bazel is the supported test setup; the assembly runner is omitted from the pseudocode. A solo developer can inspect or test the released routines with accessible development tools. Recreating the training system is a substantially different project. [Author repository](https://github.com/google-deepmind/alphadev).

### Erdős factorial divisibility: a proof generated with AI, January 2026

**Problem and contribution.** The factorial-divisibility problem asks how large a gap between sums of parameters can be under a specified divisibility relation. Its interpretation required care to exclude uninteresting cases. Sothanaphan’s January 2026 exposition presents a logarithmic-gap result and identifies its relation to earlier number theory. **Roles and chronology.** Kevin Barreto operated GPT-5.2 Pro and Aristotle. An initial formal result addressed a version that participants regarded as partial. After people clarified the intended problem, the operator requested a stronger argument and obtained another Lean proof. The exposition reports no human mathematical observation supplied for that upgraded proof. People subsequently investigated literature, simplified the formal proof, and prepared human-readable mathematics. **Assessment.** This is a public formal-proof claim with a human-checked exposition, not a benchmark exercise. The author explicitly treats absence of prior literature as a dated finding, not a proof of absolute novelty. These details come from a participant’s account, not an independent audit of all model interactions. [Mathematical exposition and provenance appendix](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.07421v1).

**Artifacts and access.** The cited Lean development is public, imports Mathlib, and is stored under a Lean-version-specific directory. It includes the range constraints in its account of the theorem. This gives an inspectable validation route without physical experiments or private datasets. It does not establish the inference cost, service access conditions, or original hardware requirements. [Public Lean proof](https://github.com/plby/lean-proofs/blob/main/src/v4.24.0/ErdosProblems/Erdos728b.lean).

**Current record and solo fit.** The community’s AI-contribution index records a full Lean solution and related prior literature rather than an entirely literature-free result. [Community attribution record](https://github.com/teorth/erdosproblems/wiki/AI-contributions-to-Erd%C5%91s-problems). The computational attempt is a close fit for an individual operating agents, conditional on model access. The documented process of establishing what was solved and contextualizing its novelty involved other people. Consequently, this example does not demonstrate an entirely collaborator-free path from selecting an unfamiliar question to establishing a publishable contribution.

### Erdős thin additive bases: valid reproving can still be rediscovery, December 2025

**Original question.** Does every density-zero set of natural numbers lie in the sumset of a set whose counting function is little-o of the square root? The maintained formal-conjecture entry says the answer is no and identifies a consequence of Erdős–Newman’s 1977 theorem as already answering it. The squares are a special affirmative case, not the whole problem. The entry is a statement stub containing `sorry`, and links separately to a proof; it must not itself be cited as a checked proof. [Maintained statement and references](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/main/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/333.lean).

**Provenance and assessment.** The community index records a December 25, 2025 full Lean solution using Claude Opus 4.5 and GPT-5.2 Pro, alongside the pre-existing full result of Erdős and Newman. It separately records an unsuccessful literature-search attempt. This is evidence of AI-assisted proof generation and formalization, but not a first resolution of an open mathematical question. Exact prompting, operator effort, original inference costs, and training-data overlap are not established by that index. [Community attribution record](https://github.com/teorth/erdosproblems/wiki/AI-contributions-to-Erd%C5%91s-problems).

**Original literature and inspectability.** The 1977 paper’s Theorem 2 gives finite-set lower bounds; the maintained statement identifies its implication for the infinite question. This report inspected the paper but did not independently formalize that implication. [Erdős–Newman original paper](https://www.renyi.hu/~p_erdos/1977-05.pdf). A later version of a separate Lean proof is publicly linked at a fixed commit. Its availability does not demonstrate that it is byte-for-byte the December artifact. [Pinned Lean artifact](https://github.com/plby/lean-proofs/blob/1d7b3f00780b85ed0462e79a1cd5650ee9055655/src/v4.29.1/ErdosProblems/Erdos333.lean). The accessible mathematical materials make study and local checking plausible for a solo operator; this case supplies no reliable estimate for a new discovery run.

## Transferable observations

These are interpretations of the cases, not product decisions:

- **Correctness, novelty, and significance are different questions.** The thin-bases case separates correctness from priority; the matrix case shows why the exact setting belongs in a novelty claim. A valid artifact does not establish that its conclusion was previously unknown.
- **Validation and discovery have different access requirements.** All four positive cases provide inspectable outputs. Some release only results, tests, or partial implementations of the discovery system. “I can verify this” is much weaker than “I can reproduce the process that found it.”
- **Human work persists around automated proof or search.** Choosing the formulation, translating outputs, finding prior work, and assessing relevance can be essential even when agents generate the decisive argument. The factorial-divisibility chronology is especially relevant to an operator learning an unfamiliar field.
- **There are useful intermediate contributions.** Better constructions, improved bounds, new proofs, formalizations, and adopted implementation improvements are distinct outcomes. A full resolution of a famous conjecture is only one form of progress.
- **An empirical test can support a real contribution.** AlphaDev’s measured performance is meaningful because it accompanies working code and documented integration. It supports claims about tested environments, rather than every workload. This review does not establish when a trained judge is sufficient to validate a new scientific result.
- **Published successes do not provide a success-rate estimate.** These cases were selected for inspectable evidence, not sampled from all attempts. The AlphaEvolve results repository itself publishes improved instances and omits matched instances. No probability of success or generic attempt budget follows from this report.

## Coverage, uncertainty, and planning implications

The bounded question is sufficiently answered: there are primary-source precedents spanning mathematics and computer systems, with identifiable roles, validation routes, public artifacts, and clear access limitations. The review deliberately includes a rediscovery so that “listed as open” is not silently treated as proof of novelty.

Coverage is strongest for proof and executable algorithm search. It does not establish precedents for learned scientific judges, public-data observational science, training small models as validators, or educational readiness checks. Those subjects belong to their own investigations; this sample supplies no basis for excluding them.

No reproduction or independent proof audit was run. Public notebooks and versioned Lean code were inspected as artifacts, not executed. The Erdős site returned access errors, so provenance was checked through the public exposition, community GitHub record, original literature, and formal-conjecture source. Mutable repositories and indexes can change after this research date. The reported histories remain dated claims rather than certification of the latest problem status.

The evidence raises two precise questions for the existing planning discussions: **what evidence is required before an opportunity or result may be called novel, and what must the operator understand well enough to check the correspondence between a problem and its claimed solution?** Neither requires a new implementation commitment; both can inform the already planned opportunity-assessment, validation, and educational discussions.

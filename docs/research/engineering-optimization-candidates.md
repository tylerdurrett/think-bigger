# Engineering optimizations whose small gains could matter

Research date: 2026-09-07. Prepared for [Which representative problems should anchor the first workflow?](https://github.com/tylerdurrett/think-bigger/issues/6). This expands the candidate exploration toward engineering; the mathematical options in [representative candidates](representative-candidates.md) remain unselected possibilities.

The examples below have documented computational or AI optimization precedents and public starting materials. They are avenues for exploration, not claims that a particular new improvement is available. Statements about solo accessibility are research assessments, not measured budgets. No optimization, training, synthesis, simulation, or hardware benchmark was run. Historical results retain their original dates; this is not an exhaustive September 2026 state-of-the-art survey.

## The matrix multiplication story

The operator's “one or two years ago” recollection most likely refers to **AlphaEvolve, announced May 14, 2025**. It found a bilinear algorithm for multiplying 4×4 complex matrices using 48 scalar multiplications instead of 49. Separately, its hardware-oriented optimizations reportedly made a matrix-multiplication kernel 23% faster and reduced Gemini training time by 1%. Those are different results: a smaller arithmetic count is not automatically a faster practical implementation. **AlphaTensor, announced October 5, 2022**, is an earlier possible match; it also searched for algorithms tailored to particular hardware. These establish precedents, not current performance guarantees. [AlphaEvolve announcement](https://deepmind.google/blog/alphaevolve-a-gemini-powered-coding-agent-for-designing-advanced-algorithms/), [AlphaTensor announcement](https://deepmind.google/blog/discovering-novel-algorithms-with-alphatensor/)

Released AlphaEvolve mathematical results include correctness-checking code, but not its search runner. Exploration could study an exact arithmetic construction or a practical kernel, with different success tests and resource needs. [Public results](https://github.com/google-deepmind/alphaevolve_results), [existing local case study](ai-assisted-contributions.md#alphaevolve-a-matrix-multiplication-construction-mayjune-2025)

## Five additional avenues

### 1. Tiny sorting and hashing routines used inside larger programs

**Concrete question.** Can a small integer-sorting routine, or a short-input hashing implementation, run faster on a specified processor while preserving its required behavior? These routines can be embedded in much larger library operations, offering a plausible distribution route for a small improvement.

**Precedent and public materials.** AlphaDev's 2023 work produced sorting routines incorporated into LLVM libc++; its authors provide assembly programs and correctness tests. The repository is archived and contains agent pseudocode rather than a complete runnable discovery system. DeepMind also reports a faster 9–16-byte hash path, and the Abseil integration is inspectable as a public commit. These are stronger adoption evidence than an isolated benchmark result. [AlphaDev paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06004-9), [released programs](https://github.com/google-deepmind/alphadev), [hashing account](https://deepmind.google/blog/alphadev-discovers-faster-sorting-algorithms/), [Abseil change](https://github.com/abseil/abseil-cpp/commit/74eee2a)

**Useful success test.** For sorting: preserve ordering, elements, type/comparator semantics, and memory safety; compare latency and the containing sort on realistic input distributions. For hashing: distinguish optimizing an existing function from designing a different hash. Matching required outputs may be necessary for the former; speed alone is insufficient for the latter, which also needs appropriate collision and distribution evidence. This is a proposed evaluation distinction, not a claim that the two problems share a ready-made proof procedure.

**Solo fit and missing evidence.** A small CPU experiment is a plausible entry point, but compiler output and instruction-set differences matter. The released AlphaDev tests document Linux/Clang support; local compatibility is untested. First identify a current library bottleneck and strong baseline, including later improvements. Novelty might concern an underserved input shape or architecture, rather than rediscovering the published routine. A microbenchmark gain needs to survive dispatch overhead, surrounding code, and maintenance/adoption review before it becomes a broad benefit.

### 2. GPU kernels that avoid unnecessary memory traffic

**Concrete question.** Can normalization, reductions, or a particular sequence of tensor operations use fewer memory transfers and kernel launches on a target GPU, without exceeding its numerical-error or memory budget? This complements matrix multiplication: the bottleneck can be moving values rather than multiplying them.

**Precedent and public materials.** KernelBench publishes PyTorch tasks, generated-kernel evaluation tools, and experiments on LLM kernel generation. Triton's fused-softmax tutorial supplies a compact example and explains why combining operations can avoid intermediate memory traffic. Its tutorial concerns a restricted family of matrices, not every shape. [KernelBench paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.10517), [official toolkit](https://github.com/ScalingIntelligence/KernelBench), [Triton example](https://triton-lang.org/main/getting-started/tutorials/02-fused-softmax.html)

**Useful success test.** Compare the actual available production baseline, including compiled or vendor implementations and matched precision; check representative shapes, strides, difficult values, memory use, and the containing application. Floating-point agreement under a stated tolerance is a bounded numerical test, not exact symbolic equality. A 2026 primary study found that stronger baselines and hidden input distributions substantially weakened apparent KernelBench wins, supporting the need to examine these details. [KernelBench-Verified](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16241)

**Solo fit and missing evidence.** One narrow kernel is a plausible software investigation with access to the target accelerator. That access, performance-measurement setup, and costs are not established here. Reimplementing a tutorial is practice; contribution requires a useful workload and a gain over contemporary alternatives. End-to-end latency or throughput, including launch and layout-conversion costs, is the bridge to impact. A faster isolated operator does not establish faster training or inference.

### 3. Smaller or faster digital adders and encoders

**Concrete question.** Can a fixed-width adder or priority encoder be implemented with less area at the same delay, or less delay at the same area, while computing exactly the same function?

**Precedent.** PrefixRL optimized parallel-prefix arithmetic circuits. NVIDIA's July 2022 account reports nearly 13,000 instances of AI-designed circuits in Hopper and describes a 64-bit adder with up to 25% less area at equivalent speed. This is a documented example where repeated circuit instances amplify an improvement; it does not imply that every smaller local circuit reduces a whole chip's power or area proportionally. [PrefixRL paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.07000), [NVIDIA's account](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/designing-arithmetic-circuits-with-deep-reinforcement-learning/)

**Public starting materials.** The EPFL benchmark suite supplies adders, encoders, and other circuits in common formats, plus best-result records. The original inputs are deliberately unoptimized; comparing only against them would overstate progress. Its contribution process checks combinational equivalence and improvements over the current best implementation. Yosys EQY provides equivalence tooling; OpenROAD offers an open synthesis/physical-design flow for a different, physical-metric assessment. [EPFL suite](https://github.com/lsils/benchmarks), [EQY](https://github.com/YosysHQ/eqy), [OpenROAD flow](https://github.com/The-OpenROAD-Project/OpenROAD-flow-scripts)

**Useful success test.** Establish functional equivalence under explicit width, signedness, and interface assumptions. Then measure either the selected logic metric, such as LUT count/depth, or mapped area/timing in a pinned flow and cell library. These metrics are not interchangeable. A proved Boolean function does not prove a fabricated device meets timing, power, or manufacturing requirements.

**Solo fit and missing evidence.** Small combinational designs can be investigated entirely in software. The original PrefixRL training was substantial, so its existence is not a personal-compute recipe. Start by understanding a small design and available proof/synthesis costs. Public-tool results could constitute a bounded design or optimization-method contribution, but transfer to an industrial process and chip adoption would require additional evidence and access. [PrefixRL compute and synthesis discussion](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/designing-arithmetic-circuits-with-deep-reinforcement-learning/)

### 4. Packing jobs or items into fewer resources

**Concrete question.** For items arriving one at a time, can a placement rule use fewer fixed-capacity bins without expensive decision-making or unacceptable failures when the input mix changes? Bin packing is a small, understandable starting model for resource utilization; actual scheduling requires additional constraints.

**Precedent and public materials.** FunSearch reported improved online bin-packing heuristics and released those heuristics with an evaluation notebook. This supports studying a compact decision rule and its generalization. Separately, Google's 2025 AlphaEvolve announcement reports recovering an average 0.7% of fleet compute capacity through a production scheduling heuristic; that real-system result should not be attributed to the simple FunSearch bin-packing example. [FunSearch paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-023-06924-6), [public bin-packing assets](https://github.com/google-deepmind/funsearch/tree/main/bin_packing), [production scheduling precedent](https://deepmind.google/blog/alphaevolve-a-gemini-powered-coding-agent-for-designing-advanced-algorithms/)

**Useful success test.** Check capacity and online-information constraints exactly, then compare bin use and decision time on withheld distributions and difficult sequences. A claimed universal competitive guarantee requires proof; a finite evaluation supports performance on its tested workloads.

**Solo fit and missing evidence.** Small simulations are likely accessible on a CPU, though no timing was measured. Before recommending an attempt, choose the real resource being modeled and establish whether one-dimensional bins capture enough of it. Memory/CPU combinations, job durations, locality, latency targets, and migration costs can alter the objective. A better toy score is not proof of cheaper cloud operation. A newly characterized heuristic could still contribute algorithmic knowledge if its claim and baselines are precise.

### 5. Compact antennas with better constrained performance

**Concrete question.** Can a compact patch or wire antenna achieve a better bandwidth, radiation pattern, or efficiency tradeoff within a specified footprint, material, and manufacturing tolerance?

**Precedent and public materials.** NASA's 2004 ST5 report describes evolutionary antenna search followed by fabrication and testing; the best designs were comparable to a human-designed mission antenna. That was computational optimization well before current language models. For accessible exploration today, openEMS supplies editable Python examples for patch, helical, and bent-patch antennas. These are starting geometries and solvers, not the original NASA search system. [NASA report](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20040152147), [openEMS tutorials](https://docs.openems.de/python/openEMS/Tutorials/index.html), [patch example source](https://github.com/thliebig/openEMS/blob/master/python/Tutorials/Simple_Patch_Antenna.py)

**Useful success test.** Specify the actual tradeoff, then assess impedance matching, bandwidth, radiation efficiency/pattern, and sensitivity to parameter variation. Check numerical convergence and relevant material, feed, and boundary assumptions. A particularly deep return-loss dip by itself does not establish a useful antenna. These are proposed investigation requirements inferred from the difference between simulation and measured-device performance.

**Solo fit and missing evidence.** A small simulation-only investigation fits the no-new-physical-experiment boundary in principle; computational cost and necessary electromagnetics learning remain unmeasured. It can support a claim about the simulated design and its robustness assumptions. Establishing real antenna performance generally needs fabrication and RF measurements, as NASA's example illustrates. The opportunity might be a better reproducible simulated tradeoff or an optimization method; deployment benefits such as range or battery life cannot be certified by software alone. A baseline matched to the same application and constraints is still needed.

## How to tell whether repetition creates worthwhile impact

The common opportunity is a useful improvement that many real workloads or manufactured devices can reuse. Frequency is only part of the assessment. For software, estimate the time/resource share affected and the achievable reduction, then account for invocation overhead and regression elsewhere. If a routine consumes 10% of runtime and its time falls by 5%, total runtime falls by 0.5% before other effects; frequent calls do not make the whole program 5% faster. For circuits and antennas, physical constraints and the design's adoption determine whether a local improvement matters.

A useful next exploration would establish the exact objective, public inputs, current strong baseline, accessible success test, target hardware or simulator, resource budget, and route from a local result to reuse. None of these avenues must be selected merely to cover a category. CPU routines and digital circuits offer close connections to exact correctness checks; GPU and packing performance needs workload evidence; antennas add a clear simulation-to-hardware boundary. Those differences make them useful discussion candidates without settling the first workflow.

# Recent AI-assisted contributions: May–September 2026

Checked 2026-09-07 for [What can we learn from documented AI-assisted contributions?](https://github.com/tylerdurrett/think-bigger/issues/2). This supplements the [earlier case studies](ai-assisted-contributions.md) with a deliberately recent selection. A September 8 addendum on the Navier–Stokes announcements was added the day they appeared; see the final section.

The strict three-month window is June 7–September 7, 2026. May is included separately because the operator specifically recalled a change beginning then. Dates below identify announcements, submissions, or documented discovery events; those are distinguished where they differ. This is a source review, not an independent reproduction of proofs or experiments, and the selection does not measure a trend or success rate.

## Two closer matches for the recalled Anthropic-employee story

The original search concentrated on Fermat. Broader searching revealed two plausible matches for the remembered simpler interaction with Claude; the exact article the operator read remains unidentified.

**Levent Alpöge and the Jacobian conjecture.** Buzzard's July 20 post describes Alpöge responding to a question from Mathew and using Fable during the World Cup final. The later human formalization demonstrates a distinct division of labor: AI-assisted discovery followed by human-written verification. [Buzzard's firsthand account](https://xenaproject.wordpress.com/2026/07/20/human-mathematicians-are-being-outcounterexampled/). Anthropic's August article identifies Alpöge as one of its mathematicians. [Affiliation source](https://www.anthropic.com/research/riemann-zeta). The public proof PR was merged July 26 and includes explicit review of correspondence to the existing conjecture statement. Its original X announcement was inaccessible to this review; the July 19–20 dating preserves local/UTC ambiguity. [Formal Conjectures review](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/pull/4474).

**Jarred Sumner and the Riemann hypothesis.** Anthropic identifies Sumner as a non-mathematician whose mathematical direction was minimal. It reports two Claude Code sessions, 31 million output tokens, extensive subagent work, numerical experiments, literature checks, and later human examination. That is a simple initiating interaction with substantial work behind it. The model was unreleased at the time. [Anthropic's August 10 account](https://www.anthropic.com/research/riemann-zeta).

The precise zeta result is an unconditional asymptotic lower bound: at least two thirds of the nontrivial zeros, counted with multiplicity, are simple and on the critical line; the optimized constant is about 0.6725. The prior record for that quantity was 5/12. This leaves the full Riemann hypothesis unresolved. The August 13 paper, revised August 19, credits Claude with discovery and Alpöge and Ralph Furman with verification and communication. [Current paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13637v2). The released Lean project documents its statement interface, analytic inputs, axiom audits, and comparator procedure; none was rerun for this report. [Formalization](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/tree/main/zeta23).

## What the other mathematical cases establish

**Grothendieck.** The example is a noncommutative finite free group scheme of order four that is not killed by four. The public PR discloses human-directed Codex and Claude assistance and provides the merged checking artifact. [mathlib PR](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/pull/41748). Buzzard reports that formalization arrived within four hours of his request and that he checked it on his laptop in under five minutes. These are verification observations, not a disclosure of discovery cost. [Firsthand account](https://xenaproject.wordpress.com/2026/07/20/human-mathematicians-are-being-outcounterexampled/).

**Maxwell.** The preprint by Philip Arathoon, Gavin Ball, and Matthew D. Kvalheim discloses GPT-5.6 Sol's construction suggestion and human mathematical verification, with Mathematica and Maple used for computation and visualization. No physical electrostatics experiment is required for the mathematical claim. This review established a public analytical argument, but no Lean certificate or journal-acceptance outcome. It should not be described as a fully autonomous proof campaign. [Full paper and tool disclosure](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27197v1).

**OpenAI's August release.** Three examples illustrate the range: a stronger high-dimensional sphere-packing upper bound; an explicit group disproving that every countable group is sofic; and a superexponential lower bound for multicolor triangle Ramsey numbers, resolving the question recorded as Erdős problem 183. These establish different things: an improved bound is not an exact answer for every finite parameter. The manuscript records an August 6 update. [Manuscript](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/ten-proofs-oai.pdf). The publisher provides [Lean files and checking instructions](https://github.com/openai/ten-proofs). A separate [Formal Conjectures entry](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/main/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/183.lean) marks the Ramsey question research-solved and links a pinned external proof. We did not compile or independently audit the ten certificates. The [announcement](https://openai.com/index/ten-advances-in-mathematics/) identifies the discovery model as internal Astra and distinguishes discovery from later manuscript preparation and formalization.

**May's unit-distance disproof.** The proof produces, for infinitely many sizes n, planar point sets with at least n^(1+δ) unit-distance pairs for a fixed positive δ. It refutes the conjectured near-linear growth without determining the exact optimum. Its AI-use statement describes automated generation and initial grading followed by human examination, rewriting, and outside expert checks. [Proof and provenance](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/74c24085-19b0-4534-9c90-465b8e29ad73/unit-distance-proof.pdf). The original model was internal and unreleased. External commentary was checked through the [announcement](https://openai.com/index/model-disproves-discrete-geometry-conjecture/); its companion remarks PDF repeatedly timed out.

## A broader scientific example, with a different access boundary

Anthropic's August 18 protein-design report describes agents operating specialist computational models and submitting designs to Adaptyv Bio and Twist Bioscience for physical testing. It reports 354 binders among 1,320 tested designs and substantial GPU allocations in some conditions. Human involvement included access approvals, infrastructure support, and arranging tests. This is a publisher-reported experimental contribution with external laboratory testing, not evidence of an effective medicine or a workflow wholly executable on a personal computer. Its companion chemical-analysis example concerns processing existing measurements; that task demonstration should be distinguished from discovery of a new scientific result. [Primary report](https://www.anthropic.com/research/Claude-accelerates-protein-design).

## Addendum, September 8: Navier–Stokes blowup claims and a priority dispute

Checked 2026-09-08, the day of both releases. This is a source review of announcements made hours earlier; nothing was compiled, and no expert assessment of either proof had appeared by the time of checking. Everything below is a dated claim by its author unless marked otherwise.

**The Clay formulation matters.** The official problem statement offers four alternatives. (A) and (B) ask for global existence and smoothness with the external force identically zero, on ℝ³ and on the torus. (C) and (D) ask for breakdown: smooth initial data and a smooth, decaying external force for which no global smooth finite-energy solution exists. A forced blowup therefore sits inside the official problem, while the unforced question that most people mean by "does Navier–Stokes blow up" is a different statement. [Official problem description](https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/navierstokes.pdf). The Clay list still records the problem as unsolved; its rules require publication, a two-year wait, and general acceptance before any award. [Rules](https://www.claymath.org/millennium-problems/rules/).

**Alpöge and Buckmaster.** Tristan Buckmaster's statement and three preprints appeared on his NYU page at about 04:00 UTC on September 8. The results are finite-time blowup with smooth forcing for incompressible porous media, for Boussinesq, and for 3D incompressible Euler on ℝ³; the Euler paper constructs axisymmetric compactly supported smooth data and a force that stays smooth through the blowup time. The statement credits the program to Diego Córdoba and Luis Martínez-Zoroa, who had obtained forced blowup with rough forcing, and describes the new work as pushing that program to smooth forcing and to Euler "with a great deal of help from LLMs": Claude, Codex with GPT-5.6 Sol, and later Astra for writeups and auditing. Progress was slow for most of a year; the Boussinesq and Euler results arrived August 15 and were verified in Lean on August 22. Buckmaster calls the first LLM-generated proof "the most horrendous I have ever read" and the Euler writeup "AI slop," released in that state under outside pressure. He also reports an unreleased hypodissipative Navier–Stokes blowup whose Lean verification was unfinished. This was a personal collaboration paid from his own research funds, not an Anthropic project. [Statement](https://cims.nyu.edu/~tristanb/statement.pdf), [Euler preprint, 112 pages](https://cims.nyu.edu/~tristanb/euler.pdf), [Boussinesq](https://cims.nyu.edu/~tristanb/boussinesq.pdf), [porous media](https://cims.nyu.edu/~tristanb/ipm.pdf). The Euler preprint's extracted text contains no tool disclosure or Lean link; the disclosure lives in the statement, and the Lean formalization is a separate public repository created at 04:03 UTC with Euler, Boussinesq, and supporting directories, no README, and no license file at the time of checking. The program it extends is public. [Announcement post](https://mastodon.social/@tristanbuckmaster/117233413705701198), [Lean repository](https://github.com/tristanbuckmaster/fluid_lean), [Córdoba and Martínez-Zoroa's forced blowup paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.22920).

**OpenAI.** "On the Navier–Stokes Millennium Prize Problem," dated September 8, says an internal system produced an analytical proof and a Lean formalization that smooth data at rest under a smooth force, with finite energy throughout, develops a finite-time singularity, establishing alternatives (C) and (D) for every positive viscosity. The same effort reports unforced Euler blowup on ℝ³ from compact smooth data. The narrative is unusually specific: a new internal model "significantly more capable than GPT-6 Astra" in training since August 28; an effort launched September 1 after rumors that two Millennium problems had been resolved; groups of coordinating agents given different variants of each problem, with the Navier–Stokes group on the order of 10,000 concurrent agents; the Euler disproof from nearly 100 agents in about 50 hours; the Navier–Stokes resolution on September 5, about 88 hours after launch; 17 further hours of Lean formalization and verification using GPT-6 Astra; 2.7 million messages and about 130 billion output tokens on Navier–Stokes alone. OpenAI says it does not intend to claim the prize. The page was blocked to direct fetching and was read through a same-day archive snapshot; its Euler paper link returned 404 from the CDN, and its Navier–Stokes writeup link was not resolvable, so the prose proofs were not inspected here. [Announcement](https://openai.com/index/navier-stokes-solution/), [archived copy](http://web.archive.org/web/20260908171518/https://openai.com/index/navier-stokes-solution/).

**OpenAI's Lean artifact is public and inspectable.** The repository was created at 10:53 UTC on September 8 under Apache-2.0. Its formalization metadata lists the two Navier–Stokes theorems and two Euler theorems with zero `sorry`, only the three standard axioms, and Comparator challenge configurations that check the proved statements against the Formal Conjectures reference formalization of (C) and (D). It records the automation as GPT-6 Astra under Codex and the review status as "self-assessed." The Navier–Stokes directory holds several hundred Lean files, some over half a megabyte. None of this was built or compared here; the metadata is the publisher's own description. [Repository](https://github.com/openai/NavierStokesAndEuler), [Formal Conjectures reference statement](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/main/FormalConjectures/Millenium/NavierStokes.lean).

**The dispute, from both sides.** Buckmaster's statement reproduces his September 3 email to an OpenAI mathematician, sent after rumors and after Alpöge was tipped that news of their work had reached OpenAI, and quotes the reply offering to "avoid competing." On September 6 he spoke twice with Sébastien Bubeck and another OpenAI representative. He reports being told of a roughly 100-page forced Navier–Stokes proof under "option c and d in Fefferman," which he has not seen; that an initial "very little human input" description gave way during the call to a team effort with an easier-problems-first plan, a Codex-written prompt, and heavy compute; that the first prompt was eventually agreed to have been sent after information about their work reached OpenAI; that his question about training on their Codex sessions, which held all their drafts, went unanswered; that two publication arrangements were proposed; that Bubeck twice asked for Alpöge's removal from authorship, citing his Anthropic employment; and the replies "Why would you ruin your career?" and "If you don't want me to be nice, then I don't have to be nice." He closes by listing what he is not claiming: he has not seen the proof, does not know what the model did, and does not know whether their data was used. OpenAI's page says the effort began September 1 after a rumor later understood to concern Alpöge and Buckmaster, that it reached out on September 6 after its own Lean verification to offer a concurrent release and to recognize their priority, that it offered visibility into its prompts and later its proof, that "no specific user data was accessed," that it "cannot rule out that de-identified data derived from their usage of our products helped improve our models," and that the proofs and even the Euler statements differ. Bubeck wrote on X on September 8 that "false and inflammatory allegations" were circulating, that he had followed academic norms, and that a fuller response would follow. Buckmaster replied on Mastodon that OpenAI's August 28 training start postdates their August 15 result and asked whether using a customer's data to scoop that customer is ethical. [Statement](https://cims.nyu.edu/~tristanb/statement.pdf), [OpenAI's account](https://openai.com/index/navier-stokes-solution/), [Bubeck's quoted reply](https://officechai.com/ai/openais-sebastien-bubeck-calls-tristan-buckmasters-claims-of-trying-to-take-credit-for-fluid-dynamics-proofs-false-and-inflammatory/), [Buckmaster's reply](https://mastodon.social/@tristanbuckmaster/117234822696475513).

**Expert commentary, before and after.** Terence Tao's September 3 thread, written before either release, describes an emerging consensus that the regularity answer is negative, sketches a four-step blowup strategy, and warns that a harness performing the whole iteration out of public view could "contaminate" the problem as a source of insight; on September 5 he added that he was "not aware of any significant developments." Within half an hour of the Alpöge–Buckmaster announcement he called it "a remarkable achievement," noted the arguments "have been formalized in Lean" and "contain significant AI input," and said Buckmaster had explained the key ideas to him by phone. He sees nothing in principle preventing extension to Navier–Stokes and "a non-negligible chance that the forcing term could be eliminated entirely," at the cost of enormous technical difficulty, and adds that he "would not be surprised if one could batter out such an extension by pouring an enormous amount of compute and AI assistance" at it, while his own interest is in the insights. His blog post summarizes the amplification mechanism and calls the papers works in progress. This is an expert reading of the ideas plus reliance on the Lean artifact, not an independent audit. Jordan Ellenberg's September 7 post makes similar points. Tao also flagged a third, independent Euler result released the same day by Ganeshram, Duruisseaux, and Anandkumar: a numerically stable unforced blowup candidate found with a physics-informed neural network, with the rigorous stability proof still lacking and only partial Lean formalization. No comparable expert reading of OpenAI's Navier–Stokes proof had appeared by the time of checking. [Tao's September 3 thread](https://mathstodon.xyz/@tao/117207849921390904), [Tao's September 8 assessment](https://mathstodon.xyz/@tao/117233527638291447), [Tao's blog summary](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/09/07/finite-time-blowup-with-smooth-forcing-term-for-the-incompressible-porous-medium-boussinesq-and-incompressible-euler-equations/), [Ellenberg's post](https://quomodocumque.wordpress.com/2026/09/07/finite-time-blowup/), [Ganeshram, Duruisseaux, and Anandkumar](https://anima-ai.org/2026/09/07/stable-singularity-of-the-euler-equations-on-r3-without-forcing/). Scientific American separately reported that experts had accused OpenAI's August release of incorporating recent literature into two of its ten results without attribution, and that OpenAI corrected its "no progress for at least a decade" wording. [Report](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/openais-latest-math-breakthroughs-commit-research-misconduct-experts-say/).

**What this adds for planning.** These are inferences from the sources, not decisions:

- Prior-work review must cover concurrent and unpublished work, not only the literature. Three groups released Euler blowup work within one day, two of them on the same forced route that another pair had opened years earlier. Rumor, preprint servers, talks, and a "who else is on this route" check belong in the review.
- Drafts placed in vendor tools are a confidentiality question for the workflow. One side put every draft into Codex sessions; the other says no user data was accessed but cannot rule out de-identified training use.
- Claim precision is the unit of novelty. Forced versus unforced, (C)/(D) versus (A)/(B), hypodissipative versus full viscosity, and "established an alternative" versus "solved the Millennium problem" are different statements, and the headlines blur them.
- A checked certificate, a readable argument, and mathematical understanding are three separate deliverables. Buckmaster wanted the human argument to be read first; Tao argues the insight from the iteration is the main value; OpenAI's review status is self-assessed.
- Status evidence on a hot problem has a shelf life of days. A leading expert reported no developments on September 5; two announcements followed on September 8.
- The accessible precedent here is the human one: an expert with an existing program, a collaborator, and commercial models over roughly a year with one month of rapid progress. The OpenAI campaign used an unreleased model and thousands of agents and is not a solo-operator template.

## Implications and remaining uncertainty

These are planning inferences from the sources:

- The recent sample includes new counterexamples, constructions, and bounds as well as formalization. It materially broadens the earlier report's historical examples.
- The Grothendieck and Jacobian cases offer especially concrete starting points for studying a compact contribution and its public checking artifact. They still involved expert problem selection or interpretation; their discovery budgets and novice reproducibility remain unknown.
- Jarred Sumner's case illustrates how a nonexpert can initiate work that produces a meaningful related result, while later expert interpretation and validation remain consequential. Discovery of a useful adjacent question belongs in the workflow's design space.
- Internal-model access, a short prompt, low human intervention, and low resource consumption are separate properties. Several impressive examples were internal-model campaigns at discovery time.
- A candidate's status and the evidence about it can change. An August 3 audit initially alleged an error in an OpenAI quantum-proof chapter; its August 25 revision withdrew the allegation after identifying a lost overbar in extracted PDF text. The revision reports no confirmed error in the examined argument, not independent validation of the full ten-result collection. Stale search snippets still repeated the original allegation. [Revised audit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14673v2).

This pass did not rerun proofs, datasets, training, or experiments, establish exact costs, or systematically search every field. The sources support the existence and reported workflows of these recent contributions. They do not supply a measured probability of success for a new candidate. The resulting choices fit the map's existing assessment, representative-problem, operator-understanding, and evidence-retention questions.

# Recent AI-assisted contributions: May–September 2026

Checked 2026-09-07 for [What can we learn from documented AI-assisted contributions?](https://github.com/tylerdurrett/think-bigger/issues/2). This supplements the [earlier case studies](ai-assisted-contributions.md) with a deliberately recent selection.

The strict three-month window is June 7–September 7, 2026. May is included separately because the operator specifically recalled a change beginning then. Dates below identify announcements, submissions, or documented discovery events; those are distinguished where they differ. This is a source review, not an independent reproduction of proofs or experiments, and the selection does not measure a trend or success rate.

## Two closer matches for the recalled Anthropic-employee story

The original search concentrated on Fermat. Broader searching revealed two plausible matches for the remembered simpler interaction with Claude; the exact article the operator read remains unidentified.

**Levent Alpöge and the Jacobian conjecture.** Buzzard's July 20 post describes Alpöge responding to a question from Mathew and using Fable during the World Cup final. The later human formalization demonstrates a distinct division of labor: AI-assisted discovery followed by human-written verification. [Buzzard's firsthand account](https://xenaproject.wordpress.com/2026/07/20/human-mathematicians-are-being-outcounterexampled/). Anthropic's August article identifies Alpöge as one of its mathematicians. [Affiliation source](https://www.anthropic.com/research/riemann-zeta). The public proof PR was merged July 26 and includes explicit review of correspondence to the existing conjecture statement. Its original X announcement was inaccessible to this review; the July 19–20 dating preserves local/UTC ambiguity. [Formal Conjectures review](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/pull/4474).

**Jarred Sumner and the Riemann hypothesis.** Anthropic identifies Sumner as a non-mathematician whose mathematical direction was minimal. It reports two Claude Code sessions, 31 million output tokens, extensive subagent work, numerical experiments, literature checks, and later human examination. That is a simple initiating interaction with substantial work behind it. The model was unreleased at the time. [Anthropic's August 10 account](https://www.anthropic.com/research/riemann-zeta).

The precise zeta result is an unconditional asymptotic lower bound: at least two thirds of the nontrivial zeros, counted with multiplicity, are simple and on the critical line; the optimized constant is about 0.6725. The prior record for that quantity was 5/12. This leaves the full Riemann hypothesis unresolved. The August 13 paper, revised August 19, credits Claude with discovery and Alpöge and Ralph Furman with verification and communication. [Current paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.13637v2). The released Lean project documents its statement interface, analytic inputs, axiom audits, and comparator procedure; none was rerun for this report. [Formalization](https://github.com/anthropics/formal-math/tree/main/zeta23).

## What the other mathematical cases establish

**Grothendieck.** The example is a noncommutative finite free group scheme of order four that is not killed by four. The public PR discloses human-directed Codex and Claude assistance and provides the merged checking artifact. [mathlib PR](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/pull/41748). Buzzard reports that formalization arrived within four hours of his request and that he checked it on his laptop in under five minutes. These are verification observations, not a disclosure of discovery cost. [Firsthand account](https://xenaproject.wordpress.com/2026/07/20/human-mathematicians-are-being-outcounterexampled/).

**Maxwell.** The preprint by Philip Arathoon, Gavin Ball, and Matthew D. Kvalheim discloses GPT-5.6 Sol's construction suggestion and human mathematical verification, with Mathematica and Maple used for computation and visualization. No physical electrostatics experiment is required for the mathematical claim. This review established a public analytical argument, but no Lean certificate or journal-acceptance outcome. It should not be described as a fully autonomous proof campaign. [Full paper and tool disclosure](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.27197v1).

**OpenAI's August release.** Three examples illustrate the range: a stronger high-dimensional sphere-packing upper bound; an explicit group disproving that every countable group is sofic; and a superexponential lower bound for multicolor triangle Ramsey numbers, resolving the question recorded as Erdős problem 183. These establish different things: an improved bound is not an exact answer for every finite parameter. The manuscript records an August 6 update. [Manuscript](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/ten-proofs-oai.pdf). The publisher provides [Lean files and checking instructions](https://github.com/openai/ten-proofs). A separate [Formal Conjectures entry](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/blob/main/FormalConjectures/ErdosProblems/183.lean) marks the Ramsey question research-solved and links a pinned external proof. We did not compile or independently audit the ten certificates. The [announcement](https://openai.com/index/ten-advances-in-mathematics/) identifies the discovery model as internal Astra and distinguishes discovery from later manuscript preparation and formalization.

**May's unit-distance disproof.** The proof produces, for infinitely many sizes n, planar point sets with at least n^(1+δ) unit-distance pairs for a fixed positive δ. It refutes the conjectured near-linear growth without determining the exact optimum. Its AI-use statement describes automated generation and initial grading followed by human examination, rewriting, and outside expert checks. [Proof and provenance](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/74c24085-19b0-4534-9c90-465b8e29ad73/unit-distance-proof.pdf). The original model was internal and unreleased. External commentary was checked through the [announcement](https://openai.com/index/model-disproves-discrete-geometry-conjecture/); its companion remarks PDF repeatedly timed out.

## A broader scientific example, with a different access boundary

Anthropic's August 18 protein-design report describes agents operating specialist computational models and submitting designs to Adaptyv Bio and Twist Bioscience for physical testing. It reports 354 binders among 1,320 tested designs and substantial GPU allocations in some conditions. Human involvement included access approvals, infrastructure support, and arranging tests. This is a publisher-reported experimental contribution with external laboratory testing, not evidence of an effective medicine or a workflow wholly executable on a personal computer. Its companion chemical-analysis example concerns processing existing measurements; that task demonstration should be distinguished from discovery of a new scientific result. [Primary report](https://www.anthropic.com/research/Claude-accelerates-protein-design).

## Implications and remaining uncertainty

These are planning inferences from the sources:

- The recent sample includes new counterexamples, constructions, and bounds as well as formalization. It materially broadens the earlier report's historical examples.
- The Grothendieck and Jacobian cases offer especially concrete starting points for studying a compact contribution and its public checking artifact. They still involved expert problem selection or interpretation; their discovery budgets and novice reproducibility remain unknown.
- Jarred Sumner's case illustrates how a nonexpert can initiate work that produces a meaningful related result, while later expert interpretation and validation remain consequential. Discovery of a useful adjacent question belongs in the workflow's design space.
- Internal-model access, a short prompt, low human intervention, and low resource consumption are separate properties. Several impressive examples were internal-model campaigns at discovery time.
- A candidate's status and the evidence about it can change. An August 3 audit initially alleged an error in an OpenAI quantum-proof chapter; its August 25 revision withdrew the allegation after identifying a lost overbar in extracted PDF text. The revision reports no confirmed error in the examined argument, not independent validation of the full ten-result collection. Stale search snippets still repeated the original allegation. [Revised audit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.14673v2).

This pass did not rerun proofs, datasets, training, or experiments, establish exact costs, or systematically search every field. The sources support the existence and reported workflows of these recent contributions. They do not supply a measured probability of success for a new candidate. The resulting choices fit the map's existing assessment, representative-problem, operator-understanding, and evidence-retention questions.
